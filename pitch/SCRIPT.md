# Kokoro · 5-minute pitch script

Lines in *[brackets]* are stage cues, not spoken. Times are cumulative targets.
Speak slowly on the problem slides; pick up pace from the solution onward.

---

## 1 · Intro · 0:00 – 0:20
*[Say nothing. Let the dark questions appear in silence. Press → to skip one if you're behind.]*
*[When the companions spell out KOKORO:]*

> Meet Kokoro. We turn your most trusted belongings into **a personal therapist you already trust**: a council of little companions, living inside the things a child carries every day.

---

## 2 · The problem · 0:20 – 0:50

> Meet Mizuki. She's fourteen, in her second year of junior high. She says she's fine.
>
> In Japan, children grow up with **gaman**: endure it quietly. So the hardest step isn't finding help. It's **breaking the silence.**
>
> *[numbers count up]* And silence has a cost. **529** student suicides in 2024, a record. **354,000** children not going to school. **1.46 million** hikikomori. And only **6%** of those who need counseling ever use it.
>
> *[point to the Japan Times clipping]* And every September, when school starts again, the numbers surge.

---

## 3 · Why no one saw it · 0:50 – 1:10

> Why did no one see Mizuki? Three walls. Saying *"I'm struggling"* feels like failing. There's no gentle first step; every service starts at therapy. And schools are blind: teachers already work the longest hours in the OECD.
>
> Monday, she skipped lunch. Wednesday, she stopped reading. Friday, late again. **Everyone saw a piece. No one saw the pattern.**

---

## 4 · Solution ① Summon · 1:10 – 2:00

> So we built Kokoro. Mizuki doesn't download a mental-health app. She uses the things she already loves.

**Step 1 · Take a photo**
> *[tap the shutter on the water bottle]* She points her phone at her water bottle and takes one photo.

**Step 2 · The photo becomes a 3D avatar**
> *[the bottle appears in 3D; drag to spin it]* Our pipeline cuts the object out of the photo, rebuilds it in 3D, and gives it a face. Her water bottle is now **Mizu**.

**Step 3 · The avatar becomes an AI agent**
> *[capture the glasses, bag and pencil case]* Each companion is its own **AI agent**, with its own personality and memory. Mizu looks after her body. **Megane**, her glasses: study. **Kaban**, her bag: school. **Fude-pon**, her pencil case: friends.
>
> *[let Mizuki talk to them]* Listen. She doesn't say *"I'm depressed."* She says *"my bag feels heavy."* And Kaban answers: *"Want to tell me what's inside? I'm listening."* Every day, her companions **listen and give her gentle advice**: drink some water, take a break, talk to a friend.

---

## 5 · Solution ② Council · 2:00 – 2:40

> And this is the heart of Kokoro: **a new way to detect depression.** No AI diary to keep, no questionnaires to fill in.
>
> Every night, the companions **meet as AI agents and talk to each other** about Mizuki's day. Her chats stay private; they share only encrypted mood signals.
>
> *[meeting plays by itself]* Mizu: she drank less. Megane: no reading. Kaban: late three days. Fude-pon: no friends mentioned.
>
> Together they ask one question: **is she showing signs of depression?** Then they **rank her risk: green, yellow, or red.** One worried companion is a bad day. **Two or more is a pattern.**
>
> And then they don't report her. **They ask her.**

---

## 6 · Solution ③ Respond · 2:40 – 3:25

> That ranking sets the level of help. Green: praise small wins. Yellow: a gentle nudge. Red: with her consent, a hand-off to the school counselor. And crisis words skip everything: hotline, 24/7.
>
> *[Watch over tab]* The school sees only the class's mood weather, never names. **Watch over, never surveil.**

**Future: VR rehearsal**
> *[click 🥽 Rehearse in VR; it plays by itself]* And here's where we're going next. Many kids want help but freeze at the moment of asking. So in **VR**, Mizuki practices first, with her companions beside her. She says *"I'm fine"*... then tries again, and her courage meter fills.

**Roadmap**
> *[click 🛠️ How we build it; point to the roadmap]* Every part exists today. Our 12-month roadmap: MVP, then the council with clinicians, a three-school pilot, and **VR rehearsal in quarter four**, leading to our first paid city contract.

---

## 7 · Why Kokoro wins · 3:25 – 3:45

> Others wait for children to speak. **Kokoro notices.**
>
> Apps mean admitting you need help: one Japanese chatbot trial enrolled 96 teens and finished with zero. Kokoro starts with her own water bottle, and *[hover a research cell]* every building block is backed by research.

---

## 8 · Business model · 3:45 – 4:10

> Who pays? **Cities.** A mid-size city pays 15 million yen a year for 5,000 students: 250 yen per child per month, less than a manga. Two contracts cover our first-year team, at around 85% gross margin.
>
> *[step through the years]* By year five: 200 cities, a million children, 5 billion yen in revenue.

---

## 9 · Social impact · 4:10 – 4:40

> What changes for one child? Without Kokoro, Mizuki stops going to school by month three, and her mom quits her job. With Kokoro, the council notices on day three. On day five, she talks to Ms. Sato. She stays in class. Her mom keeps her job.
>
> *[→ zoom out]* Then her school, a pilot city in 2027, a million children by 2031, and then the world.

---

## 10 · Close · 4:40 – 5:00

> Mizuki asked for help on a Tuesday. Her companions cheered her all the way to the counselor's door.
>
> *[door opens; pause]*
>
> Children in Japan are taught gaman: hold it in. Kokoro doesn't ask them to stop. It gives the things they already love a voice.
>
> **言えないことは、モノが気づく。** What you can't say, your things notice.
>
> Thank you.
