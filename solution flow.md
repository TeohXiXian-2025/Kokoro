# Kokoro: Solution Flow

## 心の付喪神: *"What you can't say, your things notice."*
## 言えないことは、モノが気づく

**One line:** Kokoro turns the things a child already carries every day into a council of AI companions. Together, they notice the signs the child can't say out loud.

---

## 1. The 4 issues we target (and only these)

| Issue | Data | What Kokoro goes after |
|---|---|---|
| **Low help-seeking** | **6%** counseling utilization | Children don't say they're struggling (我慢の文化) |
| **Truancy (futoukou)** | **354,000**, rising for 12 years | Early signs of withdrawal are missed, and the child has no 居場所 (place to belong) |
| **Child suicide** | **529** (2024) | The SOS isn't seen in time (命を守る) |
| **Hikikomori** | **1.46M** | Isolation that starts with losing touch with school. We prevent it at the start. |

**The shared root cause has two halves:**
1. **Children don't speak up** (gaman).
2. **Adults see the signs too late** (a busy teacher, one counselor for many schools).

Every step below fixes one of these two halves. A feature that doesn't is not in the flow.

## 3. Each step: what happens, what it solves, why it's unique

### ① 出会い Summon: *your things become your friends*

The child photographs belongings they carry every day. Each one becomes a companion with its own personality and **its own part of the child's life to look after**:

| Object | Companion looks after | Notices things like |
|---|---|---|
| 💧 Water bottle | **Body**: energy, sleep, appetite | "You barely drank today. Tired?" |
| 👓 Glasses | **Study**: focus, pressure | "We haven't read together in days." |
| 🎒 School bag | **School**: getting there, attendance | "We left late three days in a row." |
| ✏️ Pencil case | **Friends**: class, belonging | "You haven't mentioned anyone at lunch." |

- **Solves:** *Children don't speak up.* The child doesn't have to trust a stranger or a counselor. These are things they already own and care about.
- **Unique:** Other apps **assign** characters to children. Kokoro's companions **are the child's own things**, and that's where the attachment comes from.

---

### ② 日常 Ritual: *30 seconds, at a natural moment*

Each companion checks in when the child naturally uses it: the bottle at lunch, the glasses when studying, the bag after school.

- **Solves:** *6% help-seeking.* There's no appointment, no stigma and no decision to "go to counseling". Checking in becomes part of the day, like drinking water.
- **Unique:** The habit comes from the child's real routine, not from app notifications.

---

### ③ 気づき Share: *talk to the bottle, not about yourself*

The child doesn't have to say "I'm depressed." They say, *"My bag feels heavy today."* Talking about feelings through an object is the same idea as play therapy with puppets and plush toys.

- **Solves:** *Gaman culture.* It's much easier for a child to say something to their bottle than to say it about themselves to an adult.
- **Unique:** The pressure of disclosure moves away from the child and onto the object.

---

### ④ 会議 Council: *the heart of Kokoro*

Every night, the companions compare notes.

> **Example:** Mizuki's bottle noticed she stopped drinking at lunch. Her glasses noticed she stopped reading. Her bag noticed she left home late three days in a row.
> Alone, each is just a bad day. **Together, they said what Mizuki couldn't.**

- **Solves:** *Suicide and futoukou: signs seen too late.* The council catches quiet early signs that **one chatbot or one busy teacher would miss**. Because it needs 2+ companions to agree, it also raises **fewer false alarms**, so teachers trust it.
- **Unique:** **One chatbot = one witness. Kokoro = a council of witnesses.** We found no commercial product that turns a child's own belongings into companions that cross-check each other.

---

### ⑤ 寄り添い Respond: *the right help at the right level*

| Level | When | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 🟢 Green | All is well | Companions encourage and praise small wins |
| 🟡 Yellow | Early pattern | Companions gently suggest a VR practice or a small step |
| 🔴 Red | Strong pattern | With the child's consent, connect to the school counselor (SC/SSW) |
| ⚠ Crisis | Words about self-harm or suicide | **Immediate** hotline and safety protocol, **24/7**, no waiting for consent |

- **Solves:** *Child suicide.* The SOS is caught at night and at weekends, when no adult is watching. Because the child stays in control at every level except crisis, they keep trusting the companions.

---

### ⑥ 練習 Rehearse: *practice before the real thing* (練習してから本番へ)

In VR, the child practices hard moments: telling a teacher "I'm not okay", asking a friend for help, or walking back into the classroom after being absent. **Their companions stand beside them.**

- **Solves:** *Futoukou and hikikomori.* Many children *want* help but freeze at the moment of asking. Rehearsal turns "I want help" into "I asked for help", and gives children who have stopped going to school a safe first step back.
- **Unique:** The companions from daily life come into the practice space too, so the child is never practicing alone.

---

### ⑦ 見守り Watch over: *watch over, not surveil* (見守り、でも監視しない)

The school dashboard shows only an anonymous **class mood forecast** (心の天気予報), for example *"Class 2-B: cloudy this week."* No names and no individual data.

- **Solves:** *Adults see signs too late*, without adding to teachers' workload. The school sees **where** to pay attention without knowing **who**, which protects the child's trust.

---

### 🔁 The loop: *ひとりじゃない (you're not alone)*

When the child reaches out, the companions celebrate it. Companions **grow with time and care**, just like a tsukumogami gains its spirit, and a new school year or a new belonging adds a new companion.

- **Solves:** *No ibasho.* The child gets a lasting place to belong that travels with them, at school and at home, even on days they can't go to school.

## 6. Judge Q&A prep (uniqueness and real impact)

**Q: Isn't this just another AI chatbot?**
A: A chatbot waits for the child to speak. Kokoro's companions *notice*, and they notice *together*. The council is what no one else has.

**Q: Why would a child talk to a water bottle?**
A: Children already talk to plush toys, pets and their phones. Play therapy has long used puppets because talking *through* something is easier than talking about yourself. And it's *their own* bottle, not a stranger.

**Q: How does this actually reduce suicide or truancy?**
A: It fixes both halves of the root cause. Children find it easier to speak (steps ①②③), and adults see the signs earlier (④⑤⑦). In the pilot we will measure:
- daily check-in rate
- time from the first council warning to an adult being involved
- number of counselor referrals the child agreed to
- return-to-school steps completed after VR practice

**Q: Won't children get bored after a week?**
A: The check-ins are tied to things the child already does every day, and the companions grow over time. It's a relationship, not a novelty app. The pilot will measure 30-day retention.

**Q: Is this surveillance?**
A: No. The child sees everything their companions say. The school sees only the class weather. Except in a crisis, nothing about an individual reaches an adult without the child's consent.

**Q: Who exactly is it for?**
A: Students in pilot municipalities, starting with junior high school, where truancy is highest. Municipalities pay, schools use it, and children are protected.
